body:
  - Meditation is more than simply thinking quietly about Scripture. In the Bible, the Hebrew word for meditate is “hagah,” which means to ponder, reflect, and speak softly or repeatedly over something. In Greek, the equivalent is “melete,” which means to give careful attention or practice consistently. True meditation is focused attention on God's Word, allowing it to shape your thoughts, guide your decisions, and influence your actions. It is an active process of thinking, reflecting, and speaking God's Word.
  - 'Isaiah 26:3 says, “Thou wilt keep him in perfect peace, whose mind is stayed on thee: because he trusteth in thee. When your mind is “stayed on the Lord,” it means keeping your thoughts fixed on His Word instead of wandering into fear, worry, or past mistakes.” A mind stayed on God returns continually to what He says, finds confidence in His word, and remains steady regardless of life''s challenges. It is an intentional focus that produces peace in the heart. Meditation has two aspects: internal and external. Internal meditation is the reflection that happens in your heart and mind. Proverbs 4:20-22 says, “My son, attend to my words; incline thine ear unto my sayings. Let them not depart from thine eyes; keep them in the midst of thine heart. For they are life unto those that find them, and health to all their flesh.” Internal meditation strengthens understanding and allows Scripture to guide your thinking, decisions, and emotions. External meditation is the act of speaking what you are reflecting on. Speaking God''s Word aloud reinforces your focus and activates your faith. Mark 11:23 says, “For verily I say unto you, That whosoever shall say unto this mountain, Be thou removed, and be thou cast into the sea; and shall not doubt in his heart, but shall believe that those things which he saith shall come to pass; he shall have whatsoever he saith.” Speaking Gods word over your life makes your faith active and directs your mind toward the outcomes you are choosing to focus on.'
  - 'Meditation involves thinking and speaking the right things. Your focus should be on God''s Word and the life you want to live, not past failures or setbacks. Romans 12:2 says, “And be not conformed to this world: but be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind, that ye may prove what is that good, and acceptable, and perfect, will of God.” Practicing meditation daily steadies your mind, strengthens your heart, and helps you make wise decisions.'
