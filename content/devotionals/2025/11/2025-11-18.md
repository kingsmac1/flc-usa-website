title: Provide for the Needy
scripture: Proverbs 19:17 (NIV)
verse: Whoever is kind to the poor lends to the Lord, and he will reward them for what they have done.
body:
  - God's heart has always been tender toward the poor, the vulnerable, and the marginalized. In the Old Testament, He established laws that would ensure provision for those in need; not through charity alone, but through built-in systems of justice and compassion. In Leviticus 19:9–10, He commanded landowners not to harvest the edges of their fields and to leave behind any dropped grain for the poor, the foreigner, the widow, and the orphan. This wasn't just about generosity; it was about creating space for dignity, where the needy could gather food through their own efforts.
  - This command was directly lived out in the story of Ruth, a Moabite widow who gleaned in the fields of Boaz to survive. Boaz, honoring God's law, allowed her to gather freely and even instructed his workers to leave extra grain for her. Through this act of obedience and compassion, Ruth's needs were met, and eventually, her destiny was changed. This Old Testament law wasn't just about food; it was a reflection of God's justice and mercy at work in a community that cared for its own. James, the brother of Jesus, addressed the practical side of faith by calling out empty compassion. In James 2:15–16, he writes, “Suppose
  - a brother or a sister is without clothes and daily food. If one of you says to them, 'Go in peace; keep warm and well fed,' but does nothing about their physical needs, what good is it?” Real faith is not just expressed in words or feelings; it is expressed in action. Providing for the needy is not optional for the believer; it's an expression of God's love through our hands. We must remember that we are not owners but stewards. Everything we have, our finances, resources, food, and time, comes from God and is meant to be shared. When we provide for the needy, we are aligning our hearts with His. It's not about how much we give, but how much love and obedience we give with it. Whether through giving, serving, or advocating for those in need, we are participating in God's justice and mercy on the earth.
  - 'Ask yourself today: Who in your life or community is in need? It might not always be financial. Some people are starving for time, attention, or compassion. Don''t just pass by. Don''t just pray. Act! Whether it''s sharing a meal, donating clothes, giving a financial gift, or volunteering your time, your obedience can be the answer to someone''s prayer. As you provide for the needy, you''re not just helping people, you''re honoring God.'
prayer: I am a vessel of God's love and provision. I will not close my heart to the needs around me. What I have, I give freely, because I trust God to supply all my needs. My hands are open, and my heart is generous. I am God's outstretched hand of love and hope to my world. Halleluyah!
