title: Laws of Divine Restoration
scripture: Genesis 13:14-15 (KJV)
verse: 'And the LORD said unto Abram, after that Lot was separated from him, Lift up now thine eyes, and look from the place where thou art northward, and southward, and eastward, and westward: For all the land which thou seest, to thee will I give it, and to thy seed forever.'
body:
  - Restoration simply means the recovery or return of something to its original or unimpaired condition. It involves compensation or the restitution of something lost. When God restores, He not only recovers what was lost, but He also adds more to it, even in abstract areas such as joy. For instance, if someone's promotion was delayed for five years, during restoration, God backdates the promotions and compensates for the benefits, honorariums, and non-concrete things like love, excitement, and happiness (Isaiah 61:3).
  - In the earlier verses of Genesis, Abraham was wrongfully treated by Lot. He was deeply hurt by his nephew's decision, as Lot showed no regard for Abraham's rights as his master, elder, and uncle. Lot's departure caused Abraham great grief because he had given up everything in his homeland, and Lot was the only relative who had accompanied him. Due to his childlessness, Abraham might have hoped and treated Lot as
  - an heir, having adopted him after Lot’s father passed away. In Genesis 13:14-15, the Lord comforted Abraham after Lot’s departure, assuring him of all the land he would receive, despite being left with what seemed like a less desirable portion.
  - 'There are two laws of restoration you need to understand:'
  - 'Firstly, what you see matters – For restoration to take place, it''s important how you view yourself and your situation. Don’t see yourself as defeated, miserable, disappointed, or hopeless. God wants you to see yourself like Abraham did: see yourself possessing all that was lost and more. Be courageous and declare, "I’m coming out strong, I’m coming out victorious, I’m on my way to the top, I’m recovering everything, Amen!”'
  - Secondly, restoration has an appointed time, and it is a process – As seen in Abraham’s life, God’s promises were not fulfilled immediately. Abraham didn’t possess all the land at once, and his descendants didn’t fill the earth overnight. God's timing is perfect, and restoration often requires patience. Don’t rush God. Stay focused on His word, and in due time, all that He has promised to restore will come to pass
