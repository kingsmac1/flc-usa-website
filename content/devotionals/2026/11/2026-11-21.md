date: '2026-11-21'
title: Wait Patiently on God
scripture: 1 Samuel 13:11–12 (KJV)
verse: And Samuel said, What hast thou done? And Saul said, Because I saw that the people were scattered from me… therefore said I, The Philistines will come down now upon me… and I forced myself therefore, and offered a burnt offering.
body:
  - Waiting on God can become difficult when the pressure around you keeps increasing. You may know what God has said, yet circumstances begin to make you wonder whether you should take matters into your own hands. At such moments, being patient does not mean weaknesses. Patience is trusting God enough to remain obedient while you wait.
  - Saul faced that kind of pressure. Samuel had instructed him to wait, but as the Philistine threat increased and the people began to scatter, Saul became impatient. Instead of continuing to wait for Samuel, he offered the sacrifice himself. His decision may have seemed reasonable under the circumstances, but it was still disobedience. Fear convinced him that something had to be done immediately.
  - You must be careful not to make the same mistake. A delay does not mean God has forgotten you. God is never late; He knows exactly when and how to act. What looks delayed from your perspective may simply be God working according to a timetable you cannot yet see.
  - Sometimes, impatience makes you feel as though you need to “help God.” Abraham and Sarah struggled with this when the promised child seemed to be taking too long. Instead of continuing to wait, they tried to produce the promise through their own arrangement. Their experience reminds you that trying to force what God has promised can create unnecessary complications.
  - God does not need you to manufacture His answer. He wants you to trust His Word and follow His instructions. There is a difference between taking a step of faith and taking matters into your own hands because you are afraid nothing is happening.
  - 'Psalm 27:14 says, “Wait on the LORD: be of good courage, and he shall strengthen thine heart.” Waiting on God does not mean doing nothing. It means remaining faithful while refusing to move ahead of Him. When pressure rises, resist the urge to rush. Pray again. Return to what God has said. Let Him strengthen your heart. The God you are waiting on is also your Helper.'
prayer: Father, thank You because Your timing is perfect. I declare that I will not allow pressure, fear, or impatience to move me outside Your will. I trust Your Word and remain steadfast while I wait. I refuse to force doors You have not opened. My heart is strengthened, and I move only as You lead me. In Jesus' name, Amen.
