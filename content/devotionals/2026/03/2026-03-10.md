title: Standing Against The Wiles Of The Enemy
scripture: Ephesians 6:11 (KJV
verse: Put on the whole armour of God, that ye may be able to stand against the wiles of the devil.
body:
  - The word wiles as used in Ephesians 6:11 describes carefully planned, deceptive strategies of the devil. These are not random attacks, they are deliberate efforts meant to distract, weaken, and slowly erode spiritual strength. The enemy usually works quietly, relying on subtle influence rather than open confrontation, aiming to shift your focus and compromise consistency over time.
  - '1 Peter 5:8 (KJV) says, “Be sober, be vigilant; because your adversary the devil, as a roaring lion, walketh about, seeking whom he may devour.” This reveals how patient and observative the enemy is. He watches for unguarded moments: fatigue, disappointment, offense, or isolation. Thoughts such as “this doesn''t really matter” or “you can deal with this later” are examples of small suggestions designed to open the door to such influence from the enemy.'
  - 'Your ability to stand firm against these wiles requires a firm and steady posture: “Stand therefore, having your loins girt about with truth, and having on the breastplate of righteousness.” (Ephesians 6:14, KJV) There is truth is God''s Word, and it keeps your mind clear when lies attempt to distort your reality. Righteousness guards the heart against compromise.'
  - Choosing honesty when cutting corners feels easier, or responding with restraint instead of anger, are practical expressions of standing firm.
  - 'Your Faith and salvation strengthen your inner man and protects your mind. Ephesians 6:16 (KJV) says, “Above all, taking the shield of faith, wherewith ye shall be able to quench all the fiery darts of the wicked.” The fiery darts also known as Flaming arrows were a common ancient military tactic in warfare, designed to cause chaos and destruction. Spiritually, these fiery darts represent the various attacks and temptations that believers face, such as doubt, fear, discouragement and deception. These often arrive suddenly, but your faith stops them before they settle: “And take the helmet of salvation” (Ephesians 6:17, KJV). This secures identity and hope, preventing confusion and condemnation from taking root.'
  - Standing is completed through resistance by using the Word of God. You have to start speaking and acting on God's Word to directly counter deception, just as Jesus did when tempted by the devil. Stand firm by being consistent, intentional, and grounded in God's word.
prayer: Father, I thank You for equipping me to stand firm in your truth, righteousness, and faith in every situation. I remain alert and steady, refusing every form of deception in Jesus' name, Amen.
