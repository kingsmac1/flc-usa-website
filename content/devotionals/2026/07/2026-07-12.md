title: Offense Weakens Spiritual Strength
scripture: Proverbs 4:23 (KJV)
verse: Keep thy heart with all diligence; for out of it are the issues of life
body:
  - Not every spiritual battle comes loudly. Some begin quietly within the heart. You can still attend church, pray, worship, and smile outwardly while inwardly carrying offense that is slowly draining spiritual strength. This is why the condition of your heart matters deeply to God.
  - The word offense comes from the Greek word skandalon, which refers to a trap or stumbling block. That meaning is important because offense often works like a hidden trap. The enemy uses very painful experiences, harsh words, betrayal, disappointment, rejection, and misunderstandings to wound the heart. If those wounds are left unattended, they can slowly harden you inwardly.
  - Many people do not suddenly walk away from spiritual passion. Sometimes, they simply become weighed down by unresolved hurt. Their thoughts continually return to what someone said or did. Prayer begins to feel difficult. Joy fades gradually. Spiritual sensitivity weakens because the heart is occupied with pain instead of fellowship with God.
  - 'Hebrews 12:15 (KJV) says: “Looking diligently lest any man fail of the grace of God; lest any root of bitterness springing up trouble you, and thereby many be defiled.” Bitterness never remains hidden for long. It eventually affects words, attitudes, relationships, and spiritual perception. A wounded heart can begin interpreting everything through pain. Sometimes, you may feel tempted to pull away from godly relationships or become resistant to correction because offense has quietly settled within.'
  - 'Jesus warned that many would become offended in the last days. This shows that offense is one of the enemy''s subtle strategies to weaken people spiritually and create division. Forgiveness protects the heart. It does not mean the pain was insignificant, nor does it mean wrong actions are excused. Forgiveness simply means refusing to allow hurt to gain permanent control over your heart. There are moments when you must release pain because your fellowship with God is too precious to lose. Ephesians 4:31–32 (KJV) says: “Let all bitterness, and wrath, and anger, and clamour, and evil speaking, be put away from you, with all malice: And be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ''s sake hath forgiven you.”'
  - Refuse to allow offense to become a resting place within you. A healthy spirit remains full of peace, grace, and spiritual strength.
prayer: Father, help me guard my heart diligently. I refuse to live with bitterness, resentment, or hidden offense. Heal every wounded place within me and keep my spirit tender before You. I walk in forgiveness, love, and peace, in Jesus' Name. Amen.
