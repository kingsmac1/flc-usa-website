title: Be At Peace With Others
scripture: Romans 12:18
verse: If it be possible, as much as lieth in you, live peaceably with all men.
body:
  - Peace is not the absence of conflict. Peace is a condition of the heart that seeks harmony, reconciliation, and goodwill toward others. It is possible to be surrounded by people and still carry strife within. It is also possible to disagree with someone without becoming their enemy.
  - God places great value on peace. One of the realities of life is that you will not agree with everyone. People will misunderstand you. Some may offend you. Others may disappoint you. If you are not careful, hurt can turn into resentment, and resentment can slowly grow into bitterness. This is why the above opening Scripture says, “…as much as lieth in you, live peaceably with all men.” Notice that God does not place the behavior of others under your control. He places your response under your control.
  - Being at peace with others does not mean allowing people to take advantage of you. It does not mean avoiding necessary conversations or pretending problems do not exist. Peace is not weakness. Peace is choosing to respond in a way that honors God rather than allowing anger, pride, or offense to control your actions. Many conflicts continue because neither person is willing to humble themselves. Both sides want to be right. Both sides want the last word. But wisdom understands that winning an argument is not always the same as preserving a relationship. Sometimes, peace requires patience. Sometimes, it requires forgiveness. Sometimes, it requires choosing not to react emotionally.
  - Jesus was called the Prince of Peace, and His followers should reflect that same nature. Wherever Christ is allowed to rule in the heart, peace begins to influence attitudes, conversations, and relationships. A peaceful person brings calm into tense situations instead of adding fuel to the fire. This is especially important because unresolved offense affects more than relationships. It affects your spiritual life. A heart filled with bitterness struggles to enjoy the peace God desires to give. This is why Ephesians 4:31 encourages believers to put away bitterness, wrath, and malice.
  - You cannot control how everyone treats you. You cannot control what people say about you. But you can choose how you respond. The world has enough division already. Let your life be known for peace.
prayer: I declare that the peace of God rules in my heart. I will not be controlled by bitterness, offense, anger, or resentment. I choose to pursue peace in my relationships and honor God in my responses. My heart is free from strife, and my life will reflect the nature of Christ. In Jesus' Name. Amen.
