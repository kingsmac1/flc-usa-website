title: Speak Faith-Filled Words
scripture: Mark 11:23
verse: For verily I say unto you, that whosoever shall say unto this mountain, be thou removed... and shall not doubt in his heart, but shall believe that those things which he saith shall come to pass; he shall have whatsoever he saith.
body:
  - Words can sound spiritual and still carry no faith. You can repeat a Scripture because you have heard someone else say it, while your heart remains unconvinced. Jesus brought the heart and the mouth together when He said a person “shall not doubt in his heart, but shall believe.” Faith-filled words are more than positive expressions. They are words coming from a heart persuaded by God's Word.
  - The centurion in Matthew 8 understood the weight of a word spoken with authority. His servant was sick, and when Jesus offered to come to his house, the man replied, “Speak the word only, and my servant shall be healed” (Matthew 8:8). He did not ask Jesus for a sign before he would believe. He recognized that the word of Christ carried enough authority to address a situation miles away. Jesus marveled at his faith.
  - Your words should grow out of that same confidence in God. When Scripture has settled something in your heart, speak from that conviction. You do not need to force yourself to sound powerful or become louder to prove that you have faith. A quiet statement spoken from genuine conviction carries more meaning than repeated declarations your heart does not believe. Fill yourself with the Word until your speech becomes the natural expression of what has taken hold within you.
  - Pay attention to the language you use concerning the things you are trusting God for. Do not pray in faith and afterward allow frustration to supply a completely different vocabulary. When pressure increases, stay consistent with what you believe. This does not require denying what is happening. It means that what is happening does not become the source from which your words are drawn.
  - Let faith fill your heart before it fills your mouth. Spend enough time with God's Word for confidence to develop within you, and speak from that place. When your heart believes and your mouth agrees, your words are no longer empty religious expressions. They become faith-filled words carrying the conviction of someone who believes God.
prayer: God's Word fills my heart and shapes the words of my mouth. I speak from a place of confidence in God and His ability. My words agree with what I believe, and I refuse to give fear a voice through me. I speak faith-filled words over my life and everything God has entrusted to me. My heart believes, and therefore, I speak with confidence. In Jesus' Name, Amen.
