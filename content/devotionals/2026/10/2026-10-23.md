date: '2026-10-23'
title: Be Thorough In Your Choices
scripture: 1 Samuel 8:19–20 (KJV)
verse: Nevertheless, the people refused to obey the voice of Samuel; and they said, Nay; but we will have a king over us; that we also may be like all the nations.
body:
  - Wanting something badly can make it difficult to see clearly. When your heart is already set on a particular outcome, you may notice only the advantages and ignore the warnings that come with it. This is why important choices should never be made only from desire.
  - Israel wanted a king. Samuel did not simply tell them no; he carefully explained what having a king would cost them. He warned that a king would take their sons, their daughters, their fields, and a portion of what they produced. The consequences were placed before them plainly, yet they still said, “Nay; but we will have a king over us.” Their desire had become stronger than their willingness to listen.
  - You can make the same mistake when you become attached to an opportunity, relationship, purchase, position, or plan before considering where it may lead. Something can look attractive now and still carry consequences you will have to live with later. Wisdom asks more than, “Do I want this?” It also asks, “What will this require of me, and where can this decision lead?”
  - 'Proverbs 14:15 says, “The simple believeth every word: but the prudent man looketh well to his going.” A prudent person examines the road before continuing down it. You should consider how your choice may affect your future and the people connected to you. Do not dismiss concerns simply because they interfere with what you want.'
  - This also means being willing to hear counsel that challenges you. Sometimes, the advice you least want to hear is the very advice that deserves your attention. If several trustworthy voices are raising the same concern, do not rush to silence them. Take time to pray and examine the matter honestly.
  - There is wisdom in thinking beforehand. It is far easier to reconsider a decision before you make it than to spend months or years repairing avoidable consequences. Do not allow desire to make you careless. Look beyond what feels good today. Ask God for wisdom, consider the cost, and give yourself enough time to make a sound choice.
prayer: Father, thank You for giving me wisdom through Your Word. I declare that I make careful and thoughtful choices. My desires will not blind me to truth, and I will listen when wise counsel brings needed correction. I examine my steps and make decisions with understanding. In Jesus' name, Amen.
