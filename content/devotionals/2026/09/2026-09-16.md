title: Make Good Use Of Your Time
scripture: Colossians 4:5
verse: Walk in wisdom toward them that are without, redeeming the time.
body:
  - Tiime is one of those gifts whose value is often realized after it has passed. Money lost can sometimes be recovered. An opportunity missed may occasionally return. But yesterday will never be available to you again. This makes the way you use your days an important part of your stewardship before God.
  - Ephesians 5:16 NIV says, “….Making the most of every opportunity, because the days are evil.” The thought behind redeeming is to make full use of something valuable while it is still available. Interestingly, the Greek word used here for time is kairos, which often refers to a fitting or opportune moment. Paul was not only concerned about hours passing on a clock. He wanted us to recognize the opportunities contained within those hours.
  - The conversation you have with someone today may become an opportunity to encourage that person or speak about Christ. The skill you have been postponing learning may be important for an opportunity ahead. There may be something God has placed in your heart that you keep pushing into another week because you assume there will always be more time. Wisdom recognizes when a moment deserves action.
  - It is also possible to be extremely busy while making poor use of your time. A full schedule does not automatically mean a fruitful life. Sometimes, the problem is not that you need more hours; you need to become more deliberate about what receives the hours you already have. Think about how easily ten minutes on your phone becomes an hour. Small distractions repeated every day eventually consume large portions of your year.
  - This does not mean every moment must be filled with work. Rest has its place. Time with people you love matters. Even Jesus withdrew from the crowds at times. Wise stewardship is not about living hurriedly; it is about knowing what deserves your attention in each season and refusing to let less important things continually displace it.
  - Moses prayed, “So teach us to number our days, that we may apply our hearts unto wisdom” (Psalm 90:12). There is wisdom in remembering that your days have value. You do not need to live anxiously about time, but you should live intentionally. Give proper attention to your walk with God. At the end of your life, the question will not be how busy you managed to become. What will matter is what you did with the life God gave you.
